# Parkour, shooting, and cover

Recorded: 2026-09-12

Status: Future idea — save for much later.

## Core idea

Combine the jumping and parkour side of the game with shooting, so choosing
and executing a movement route creates an opportunity to hit an enemy.
Being able to shoot during the double-jump animation is the starting point
that made this possibility interesting.

Enemy positions and cover could give the player reasons to move around a
fight vertically, using the movement abilities they already know.

## Where it could fit

This is an option for selected encounters and levels, not a requirement for
every shootout. Some fights can stay straightforward.

Tall vertical levels could make particularly good use of the idea, offering
different routes and a choice of how to approach enemy positions. A player
might climb toward a firing angle, take a lower route, or use a wall jump to
reach an exposed side. The layout would give room to choose an approach
instead of requiring the same manoeuvre each time.

## Examples to explore

- Double-jump over an enemy's cover to reach an exposed firing angle. Include
  opportunities to shoot an enemy from below, depending on the height of their
  position and the route around the obstacle.
- Travel underneath an enemy position, then wall-jump up behind them and fire
  from the exposed side.
- Link a jump, an airborne shot, and a wall jump into a continuous movement
  sequence through an encounter.

These are examples of the interaction, not fixed encounter layouts. The
interesting part is finding an opening through movement and shooting during
that opening.

## Cover as a later ingredient

Some objects could protect from one direction while leaving the enemy exposed
from another: the front might be protected while the underside, top, or rear
can be reached by a different route.

The exact cover rules are secondary for now. Whether this comes entirely from
the shape and placement of objects or eventually uses special directional
behavior is still open.

## Questions for a future experiment

- Can the player read the protected side and the useful route at a glance?
- Does the exposed moment leave enough time to aim and shoot while moving?
- Can different movement routes offer different ways through the same fight?

When this idea is revisited, a small encounter with one covered enemy and an
upper or lower route could be enough to explore the interaction.

## Related possibilities already mentioned

Slow motion / bullet time during airborne shooting, and ways of chaining
double jumps with shooting, were also raised as possible later directions.
They remain separate possibilities; this core movement-and-cover idea does
not depend on adding them.
